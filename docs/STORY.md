# SUPER ZERO — story draft

Status: fourth draft, September 11, 2026. Decisions from the back-and-forth are marked **decided**. Nothing here is built.

## 1. The pitch in one breath

Alien saucers are over every major city on Earth, lifting people out of the streets in beams of light. Over Fairhaven, they are lifting more than anywhere else, and one man in a research compound thinks he knows why. The task force sends the one asset it can spare to get him and what he found. That asset is Zero.

## 2. Zero

- **Decided.** Known to the military and the government, a name people in uniform have heard. A 007 who is not as cool or as professional. He has the confidence and the results; he does not have the polish.
- Confident, cheerful, physically unstoppable, and a bit dim. Sure of himself in a way that keeps being rewarded.
- Solves problems with force and is delighted when it works, which is always.
- Likes people. Polite to civilians, apologetic to property.
- Never asks why the aliens are here. It does not occur to him.
- Action-movie lines, slightly tilted. Not parody.
- **Decided.** The beams can take him. He does not know that yet and neither does the player. That is the ticket up into a saucer later.

Rule: the joke is never that Zero is an idiot. The joke is that the world keeps proving him right.

## 3. The Commander

- **Decided.** Military, strict, clean, professional, and very tired of Zero. She runs retrieval from the joint task force, far from Fairhaven.
- She briefs properly once, in the cold open. In the level she speaks only when something changes. Radio silence is the default.
- She never says he is wrong when he is right. She says "...fine."
- She has rank and a name; the player may never hear either, because there are only two people on the channel. "ZERO." is her emphasis.

## 4. The Doc

- **Decided.** Dr Patel, six doctorates, insists on Professor. Zero calls him Doc from the first line and never stops.
- Runs a research compound attached to Fairhaven University. Noticed the saucers concentrating over Fairhaven and connected it to the device recovered from Antarctica in 2003, which is sitting in his compound.
- He is the game's explainer. The player learns what the invasion is in his words, later, in fragments, mostly while he is being carried.
- Not heard in Level 01, except possibly one crackly half-line at the end so the player knows he is alive.

## 5. Fairhaven

- **Decided.** A large metropolitan city, Nashville-sized. Big, important, not that important. That is exactly why it is the one the task force could spare Zero for.
- The mayor is a bumbling idiot worried about his outlook. He is heard, never seen: an emergency broadcast looping on kiosk radios and shop TVs. "Fairhaven remains open for business." One gag, placed once per level at most.
- Municipal texture on the ground: District 04, Canal Street, Route 12, Municipal Works Yard. Federal texture on the radio. The two rubbing together is a joke that needs no lines.

## 6. The world

- **Decided.** Global. Saucers over every major city. The superpowers are engaged and stretched. Fairhaven is not a priority to anyone except Patel, and through him, the task force.
- **Saucers as a world system.** Large UFOs cross the sky at distance, slow and high. Every so often one stops and drops a vertical beam onto the city. Things go up the beam. Over Fairhaven there are more of them than a city this size deserves. In Level 01 they stay over the backdrop skyline, never over the route. Later levels bring a beam onto the route, and eventually Zero goes up one.
- Ground aliens are the squishies and their bigger cousins. Nobody explains them in Level 01.
- Signs of the invasion: smashed shopfronts, abandoned cars, cordons set up and left, smoke, pigeons where people should be. No bodies. A mug left on a checkpoint counter.
- The city keeps functioning in small stupid ways: marquee bulbs chasing, the mayor's broadcast, a "NO STOPPING" sign where everything has stopped.

## 7. The job

- **Decided.** Zero is not there to save the city. He is there to retrieve the asset: Patel and the Antarctic device. The city is in the way.
- **Decided.** Acts. Act 1 is Fairhaven: get the McGuffin. Act 2 takes it to the scientists for testing and defends it. Later acts are open. Patel is not heard until the act where the player reaches him.
- **Decided.** Levels end somewhere that can load out: a door closes, a radio cutscene plays, the next level loads. Level 01 ends at a secure elevator.
- Level 01 is the route from the crash through District 04 to the **Fairhaven University Museum**, the university-owned building where the Antarctic device was first catalogued in 2003. Its lobby is the level's second interior. The secure freight elevator at the back goes down to the research levels. Doors close, radio, load.
- Level 02 is underground: the research levels and whatever the university built down there since 2003.

## 8. Cold open

Black, then a helicopter crossing the city's own sky shader, rotor spinning, nothing else animated. Sixty to ninety seconds of radio. This is the only place the two of them talk at length.

Shape, not final lines:

- Commander gives a real briefing, and she is good at it: the global picture in three sentences, why Fairhaven, Patel, the device, the museum's last contact, then the ground truth. "There are UFOs actively searching and abducting. We have roving bands of armed hostiles in the streets. The first unit is down. We are landing you at their crash location. Any questions?"
- Zero: "Yeah. This city have a good pizza place?"
- Beat. She answers it, flat, because she has learned it is faster: "There's a Sal's on Canal Street."
- Zero: "Great."
- Commander: "Do you understand what that means?"
- Zero: "Nope."
- Shotgun racks.
- Zero: "Where do you want me, boss?"
- Cut to the crash.

Sal's sits on Canal Street in Level 01, shutters down. Zero passes it. One word, or nothing. That is the only callback.

## 9. Level 01 beats (playtest slice, about five minutes)

The beats below are the five-minute playtest slice. Mission 01 as shipped targets ten to fifteen minutes, so the slice is about the opening third; two more chunks of comparable size sit between the plaza and the museum lift in the finished mission.

Radio budget for the whole level: about four exchanges. The cold open did the talking. Jokes below are placeholders to be judged against the built level.

## 10. Rules the level must obey

1. **Roads go somewhere.** Every road turns, meets a T, or ends in a cul-de-sac. Never a dead end into a wall or a pedestrian area. Lane paint never runs past the road.
2. **You can see the city continue.** Gaps show cross streets with parked cars, a bus, a distant cordon. Blocked side streets are blocked by something plausible.
3. **Pedestrian spaces look pedestrian.** Different paving, bollards, planters, no centre line, no kerbs.
4. **Side routes exist and pay off.** Fire escapes and parkour to a roof, a shop with a busted door and a pickup inside, an alley shortcut. Ten to twenty seconds each, always something back.
5. **Kickable doors share one look.** One colour, one frame, taught by the first door's prompt. Metal shutters are never kickable.
6. **Cordons are vehicle scale.** Never a pedestrian gate on a road.
7. **Mostly exterior, a couple of interiors.** One shop and one lobby in Level 01.
8. **The sky is part of the level.** Saucers and beams visible somewhere from the route at all times.

## 11. Difficulty ladder

- Competent player takes damage, might die once at the plaza, finishes the slice in about five minutes and the finished mission in ten to fifteen.
- Beginner who has never used a mouse dies a few times. Deaths before the cordon are a design bug.
- Health crates before and after each fight, not during. Shotgun ammo generous.
