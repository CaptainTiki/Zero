# SUPER ZERO — story

Status: fifth draft, September 15, 2026. This replaces the alien-abduction draft. The premise
below is the adopted direction. Earlier candidates (global abduction invasion, childhood
monsters, artifact hunter) are dead; see the end of this doc for what was kept from them.

## 1. The pitch in one breath

Aliens are quietly terraforming Earth, running the operation behind ordinary businesses and
truly terrible disguises. Zero is an agent of the organisation that deals with this. He is sent
to a factory that has been smogging out the city, and it turns out the factory doesn't make
anything at all.

## 2. Tone

- **Defenders of Earth.** Cartoony and silly, but not a cartoon. The world is played straight
  and the aliens are what's absurd.
- **The comedy is in the world, not in the hero's mouth.** Signage, props, disguises and alien
  procedure carry the jokes. Nothing winks at the camera.
- **Zero stays mostly quiet.** He is not a quipping action hero. He looks at something ridiculous
  and shoots it.
- **Keep it deadpan.** The aliens are trying very hard and are completely certain they are
  getting it right.

## 3. Zero

- An agent of the organisation. Known inside it, unremarkable outside it.
- Confident, fearless, physically unstoppable, and not bright. Solves problems with force and is
  delighted when it works, which is always.
- Says little. The joke is never that Zero is an idiot; it is that the world keeps proving him
  right.
- He wins because he walks straight through gaps a clever person would talk themselves out of.

## 4. The Commander

- Runs the operation over the radio, far from the site. Strict, clean, professional, very tired.
- She briefs, then goes quiet. In-level she speaks when something changes, or to point Zero at a
  detail worth looking at. Those requests are the investigation beats.
- She never says he is wrong when he is right. She says "...fine."

## 5. The enemy

- **Aliens, terraforming Earth.** They are not here to exterminate anyone. They are making the
  place suit them, one system at a time: the air first, then whatever comes after.
- **They hide behind ordinary businesses.** A factory, and whatever fronts follow it.
- **Every alien is called John.** They learned that humans have first names, picked one, and
  applied it correctly to all of them. Badges, parking spaces, the employee of the month wall,
  the PA system. The joke is never explained. Taken from Buckaroo Banzai.
- **The disguises are the running gag.** Cardboard cutout employees, smiling and waving and
  pretending to operate machinery. Signage that is confidently wrong: HOOMAN WORKING STATION.
  The building itself looks like a perfectly normal factory. Only the details are insane.

## 6. The mastermind

- Unnamed so far, and deliberately not written yet.
- **Super smart, misses key details.** Genuinely dangerous and genuinely competent. The
  terraforming works. He simply cannot see the obvious, and he tries too hard, which is how the
  mistakes happen.
- His blind spots are the level's clues. Every joke in the dressing is also something Zero can
  investigate or exploit.
- He probably does not speak for the first level or two. When he finally tells Zero to stop what
  he is doing, that should be an event.
- Open: his name, and the one thing his perfectionism fixates on. Not needed to build levels.

## 7. Level 1 — Factory Investigation

Reports of a factory smogging out the city. Zero arrives at the parking lot and goes in.

1. **Parking lot and entrance.** Empty cars, all parked perfectly. Every space marked John.
2. **Reception and offices.** The employees are animated cardboard cutouts, smiling, waving,
   pretending to work. Signage confidently wrong.
3. **Factory floor.** A few aliens attack. Resistance grows as they realise Zero is not buying
   the disguise. The Commander asks him to look at specific things.
4. **The machine.** The factory makes nothing. It is a smog plant, pumping chemicals into the
   atmosphere as part of the terraforming.
5. **Blow it up.**
6. **Timed escape.** Not hard, but enough to make the player run, and to make them miss a secret
   on the way out. That missed secret is the hook for a second run.

A property records check names the university as the building's owner. Command redirects Zero
down the street, which is Level 2.

## 8. Level 2 — the city

The existing city greybox, `scenes/levels/l01_district04.tscn`. It survives the pivot. It needs
its secrets relocated and hidden properly, its scenery dressed, and a couple more kickable doors
with offshoots. See `docs/LEVEL01_PLAN.md`.

## 9. Rules a level must obey

1. **Roads go somewhere.** Every road turns, meets a T, or ends in a cul-de-sac. Never a dead end
   into a wall or a pedestrian area. Lane paint never runs past the road.
2. **You can see the world continue.** Gaps show more of it. Blocked routes are blocked by
   something plausible.
3. **Pedestrian spaces look pedestrian.** Different paving, bollards, planters, no centre line.
4. **Side routes exist and pay off.** Ten to twenty seconds each, always something back.
5. **Kickable doors share one look.** One colour, one frame, taught by the first door.
6. **Secrets are never signposted.** If it is obvious it is not a secret, it is an ammo pickup.
   Twelve per level is the upper limit.
7. **Mostly one environment per level, with a couple of interior surprises.**

## 10. Difficulty and length

- Run length band is 8 to 15 minutes. Eight is the low end of acceptable, fifteen is the limit.
- A normal player should end around 50 to 60% of everything. Speedrunners score lower.
  Completionists take as long as they like.
- A competent player takes damage and may die once at the climax.
- Par is roughly three times the headless route test's walk time.
